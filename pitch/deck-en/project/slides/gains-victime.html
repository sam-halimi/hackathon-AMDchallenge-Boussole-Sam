For survivors: fewer after-effects, rights sooner

Mental health

74.6%

of survivors have post-traumatic stress one month later. Acting early reduces its severity (g = −0.23).

Boussole: inform and refer from the first hour, without forcing the story.

Physical health

72 h

for HIV prevention and DNA. Missed deadlines can’t be made up.

Boussole: every deadline shown with its source, at the right time.

Rights

30 days

for 69.8% of IVAC decisions when the file is complete, versus 93.6 days on average.

Boussole: a complete file, ready if they decide.

A 17-minute video during the exam clearly reduced acute stress (odds ratio 0.13, pilot trial, JAMA Network Open, 2026). Informing early helps; the pilot will measure Boussole’s effect.

Sources: Dworkin et al., TVA 2023; Oosterbaan et al., EJPT 2019; Walsh et al., JAMA Netw Open 2026 (N = 50); Quebec Ministry of Justice, LAPVIC annual report 2024-2025, p. 79; Canadian guideline, CMAJ 2025

Three benefits for the person. The strongest number is the IVAC one: when the file is complete, seven decisions in ten come in under 30 days, instead of 94 days on average. IVAC is Quebec’s crime victim compensation program. Caution on the video: it is a 50-person pilot trial, and an older study (Resnick 2007) found mixed effects depending on prior history. Boussole is not a video: these studies say that informing early helps, not that the app is already proven.
