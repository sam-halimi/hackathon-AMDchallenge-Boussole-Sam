Mental health

Cautious assumptions: 2 points less post-traumatic stress at one year; 40% of survivors claim IVAC, processed 20% faster; one fewer consultation for 30% of survivors.

Break-even for the State

1 survivor in 240

Sparing a single survivor in 240 one year of post-traumatic stress is enough to pay for Boussole.

For comparison: sexual assaults from a single year cost Canada $4.8B (2009). One year of PTSD costs about US$19,630 per person.

Calculations: pitch/recherche/modele_economique.py. Sources: Davis et al., J Clin Psychiatry 2022; Justice Canada (Hoddenbagh et al., 2014); Quebec LAPVIC report 2024-2025; FIQ nurse pay scales 2025-2026. Amounts in Canadian dollars unless noted.

This is a model, not a result: every assumption is stated plainly and will be measured during the pilot with a research partner. The message for the State is the threshold: Boussole costs about $81 per survivor; sparing one survivor in 240 a year of post-traumatic stress is enough for the State to break even, without counting the IVAC, Rebâtir and nursing time. The PTSD cost is a 2018 US figure, kept as is in Canadian dollars: that is cautious.
