Time saved, according to the studies

35.3%

of nursing time goes to documentation

Hendrich et al., Permanente Journal, 2008 · 767 nurses

−24%

of that time with an electronic record, at the bedside or the station

Poissant et al., JAMIA, 2005 · review of 23 studies

3 h

median time for the initial exam and evidence collection (1 to 8 h)

Ciancone et al., Ann Emerg Med, 2000 · 61 programs

Our estimate: 3 h × 35.3% × 24% ≈ 15 minutes given back per exam. A calculation from these three studies, to be measured during the pilot.

PubMed page screenshots. doi:10.7812/tpp/08-021 · doi:10.1197/jamia.m1700 · doi:10.1016/s0196-0644(00)70053-9

Three studies, three numbers, one multiplication. Hendrich: documentation takes more than a third of nursing time. Poissant: an electronic record cuts that time by about a quarter. Ciancone: an exam takes about three hours. Together, about 15 minutes given back to the patient per exam. Be clear: no study has measured this gain for the forensic kit; it is an estimate the pilot will check.
